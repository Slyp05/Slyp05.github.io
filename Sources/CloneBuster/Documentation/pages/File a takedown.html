File a takedown notice

Send a notice to the store that hosts a copy, and what to check before you do.

Before you send a notice

A takedown notice is a legal statement, and a false one can make you liable. Before you send one, check the following:

  • The work is yours: you made it, or you hold the rights to it.
  • The copy isn't licensed from you, for example through a publishing deal or an asset you sold.
  • The copy takes your actual content, such as your art, music, text, levels or code, and not only your mechanics or your genre.

If you're unsure, ask a lawyer who handles copyright.

Send the notice

  1. Export a proof of the files the copy takes from, with the copy's listing URL and a screenshot.
  2. Upload the export folder somewhere a reviewer can open without an account, such as Google Drive or Dropbox with link sharing on.
  3. Open Notice text.txt, replace [paste the link to this folder] with the link, and check the rest of the paragraph.
  4. Open the store's notice form, paste the paragraph into its description field, and fill in the other fields.

Don't send original files to the store: anything it receives can reach the copier.

Where each store takes notices

  • Steam: Notice of Copyright Infringement
  • Google Play: Report Content On Google
  • App Store: App Store Content Dispute
  • itch.io: itch.io's terms, which give the contact for copyright notices
  • Epic Games Store: Reporting Intellectual Property Infringement

If a link has moved, search the store's help pages for "copyright infringement".
